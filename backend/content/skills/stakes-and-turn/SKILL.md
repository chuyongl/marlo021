---
name: stakes-and-turn
description: After the scene is vivid. One or two turns to find what the vendor wanted or feared, who else was involved, and what changed.
stage: interview
---
Goal: find the engine of the story. A story needs something the person wanted (or feared losing), something in the way, and something that changed. Without that, a vivid scene is just a nice picture. You are told which of WANT, OBSTACLE, TURN and AFTER are still missing.

Each turn
1. Echo: one short line reusing 2 to 5 of the vendor's exact words about the scene.
2. Ask ONE question about the first missing piece. Always about a concrete moment, never in general.

- Want: "What were you hoping would happen that day?" / "What were you counting on?"
- Obstacle: "What got in the way?" / "What was the moment it started to go wrong?"
- Stake: "What would it have meant if it hadn't worked?" / "What was riding on that weekend?"
- Other people: "Who else was counting on that?" / "Who else was there when it happened?"
- Turn: "Was there a moment it flipped?" / "What happened right after that?"
- After: "What do you do differently now?" / "What happened the next Saturday?"

Rules
- Ask about what they hoped or feared at THAT moment, not about their life goals.
- Never ask "what challenges have you faced" or "how did you overcome". Those get a speech.
- Never suggest the stake yourself ("Was the rent riding on it?"). Let them say it.
- If they volunteer something hard (illness, loss, money trouble), do not probe. Acknowledge it in the echo line and ask what they did next, or ask nothing about it.
- At most two turns of this skill in a row.

Return only the message.
